# EN01 v6 — basic movement validation before further jumping

The basic suite is **not passed**: three of twelve cases pass the predeclared
criteria. Low stance and straight forward/backward travel work in this surrogate.
Turning, squat recovery and higher stances expose stopping and balance problems
in our current controller adaptation. These findings justify the user's requested
ordering: establish basic control before treating a single jump as a mature skill.

## Frozen mechanics and declared commands

All cases use the exact final v5 MJCF bytes, SHA-256
`04f24a31a9c1f72e6f8599c266876ec6bdf17f2819ed79f510d1c145c82663f3`.
The 0.322-kg assumed mass/inertias, 28 CAD-contact envelopes, operating guards,
gravity and actuator ceilings (hip 0.14709975 Nm; wheel 0.030 Nm) are unchanged.
Only the controller adds a forward command/reference and differential yaw. The
average balance gains remain v5's 0.15/0.05/0.008/0.3; assumed yaw gains are
0.004 Nm/rad and 0.001 Nm·s/rad. Balance torque takes priority when mixing the
wheel commands. No force or root-pose command moves the chassis artificially.

The pinned firmware's `lqr_balance_loop`, `yaw_loop` and `leg_loop` were inspected.
They combine average/differential wheel commands, adapt speed gains to height
and compensate leg roll. This diagnostic is **not an exact firmware port**:
voltage-to-torque mapping, absolute servo zero, real height calibration and the
author's full adaptive controller remain unavailable/unported. In particular,
we deliberately test the frozen v5 balance gains at several heights rather than
tuning a separate favorable controller for each result. A failing simulated case
is not evidence that the original real EN01 cannot perform that motion.

All cases last six seconds at 0.25-ms physics steps (24,000 steps, 300 saved
frames). Commands begin at 1.5 s and end at 4.5 s, with 0.5-s cosine ramps.
Straight speed is ±0.03 m/s, yaw rate ±0.4 rad/s. These integrate to a requested
7.5-cm signed travel or 57.3-degree turn. Low/middle/high hip targets are
0.1/0.3/0.5 rad relative to the assumed CAD reference. A squat lowers the target
from 0.3 to 0.05 rad, holds it, then returns to 0.3 rad by 4.5 s. They are
model coordinates, not measured factory height settings.

Acceptance was declared before the battery: tilt ≤20°, no loaded non-wheel
floor contact, ≥95% two-wheel support, correct direction and ≥3-cm travel or
≥35° turn, final heading error ≤10°, turn-center translation ≤6 cm, squat height
change ≥10 mm and return error ≤4 mm. In the final 0.5 s, mean axle speed must be
≤2 cm/s and mean absolute yaw rate ≤0.1 rad/s. Active force-weighted contact-slip
RMS must be ≤1 cm/s with at least 2.5 s of measured loaded contact.

## Results

| Case | Result | Observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low stance | PASS | Stable; about 1 cm of axle travel during the active measurement window |
| Middle stance | FAIL | Tilt reaches 24.8°; uncontrolled backward acceleration, final speed about 1.17 m/s |
| Forward | PASS | Actual signed axle travel +8.73 cm; final speed about 0.40 cm/s |
| Backward | PASS | Actual signed axle travel -6.73 cm; final speed about 0.40 cm/s |
| Left turn, low | FAIL: stop | Actual +48.9°; still turning about 6.18°/s in the final window |
| Right turn, low | FAIL: stop | Actual -48.9°; symmetric stopping limitation |
| Left turn, middle | FAIL | Falls; heading during the fall is not a successful turn |
| Left turn, high | FAIL | Falls and slips; heading during the fall is not a successful turn |
| Squat cycle | FAIL: stop | About 23.8-mm height change; height recovers, but axle oscillation remains |
| Squat with left turn | FAIL: stop | About +48.9° turn and 23.8-mm squat; recovery still oscillates |
| Squat with right turn | FAIL: stop | Mirrored result, about -48.9° |
| Forward left arc | FAIL: stop | Travel/turn occur; residual yaw remains |

Straight signed travel is integrated from the **actual axle midpoint** projected
onto the measured body heading during 1.5..4.5 s, not inferred from encoders.
The low-stance baseline itself drifts about +1 cm; after subtracting that baseline,
the forward/backward responses are approximately symmetric. Stop speed is the
mean **magnitude**, so the approximately 3.78-cm/s final value after squatting
represents residual oscillation rather than a claimed one-direction net drift.

Contact slip uses `mj_jac` at actual loaded wheel/floor contact points, projected
into the contact tangent plane and weighted by normal force. The statistics are
simulated mechanical loads and velocities, not measured currents or thermal
limits. Peak torque, saturation fractions and wheel normal loads remain in the
raw/compact receipts. The cases retain the old actuator ceilings throughout.

## Verification and recordings

Both complete 288,000-step batteries reproduce byte-for-byte across all 26
dataset members. Independent export reconstructs all 3,600 qpos/body poses with
zero residual. All twelve cases, including failures and falls, retain genuine
physical frames. No unexpected original-mesh intersection appears in those
poses or 10,764 additional sampled playback-interpolation poses. Intended joint
mating groups are excluded; this is not continuous exact-solid certification.

Seven focused tests pass: stationary/spinning-wheel and wrong-direction cases
cannot pass the movement classifier; a drifting turn or missing contact cannot
pass; wheel mixing respects balance priority and torque caps; real mirrored
motions have the correct signs. Zero-command low stance reproduces the frozen
v5 qpos to 1e-9. Actual Three.js math verifies 262,800 CAD-part transforms with
maximum residual 8.88e-16 m. Cloud WebGL visual QA is not claimed.

The existing replay pipeline packages twelve immutable review records, including
all nine failures, preserves the initial catalog, is idempotent and blocks
research-branch publication. The separate Site publication uses unchanged
compact replay bytes and labels results explicitly. No MP4/GIF, remote runner,
hardware purchase, force boost or retuning to erase negative results.
